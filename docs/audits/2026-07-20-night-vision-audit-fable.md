## 1. Direction verdict — held in tension

The stated vision under test: ai-council is a **creativity engine** — a foreign repo triggers a multi-model debate that does online research when it doesn't know something, reduces cognitive bias, and reaches the right decision it can act on. Three themes: CLI seats for cost, delegation surface + advisor, non-cognitive-bias debate.

### The strongest case AGAINST the direction

1. **Matched-compute evidence is against naive multi-agent debate.** The 2025–2026 literature repeatedly finds that multi-agent debate (MAD) "fails to consistently outperform simpler single-agent strategies, even with increased computational resources," and that at equal response counts MAD *underperforms* plain self-consistency majority voting ([ICLR 2025 MAD blogpost](https://d2jud02ci9yv69.cloudfront.net/2025-04-28-mad-159/blog/mad/)). A 2026 line goes further: "isolated self-correction prevails over unguided homogeneous multi-agent debate" ([arXiv 2605.00914](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.00914)). The repo *knows* this — BACKLOG #55 (the council-arch G5 gap) admits the Council has never been checked against a single-strong-model or self-consistency baseline — and #55 remains unbuilt. The direction is currently an unfalsified bet.
2. **Reasoning models are internalizing debate.** 2026 work shows frontier reasoning models implicitly simulate multi-perspective deliberation inside one trace ("societies of thought", [arXiv 2601.10825](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.10825v1)), and distillation lines (Debate-Reflect-Distill, SMoA) explicitly aim to collapse multi-agent orchestration into a single model ([survey via arXiv 2601.22297](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2601.22297), [Mixture of Debaters, arXiv 2606.29425](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.29425)). If that trend holds, an external 5-model debate becomes an expensive re-implementation of what one $X call does internally.
3. **The "research when it doesn't know" half is commoditized.** Deep research is now a standard feature of every major assistant; purpose-built agentic researchers (Parallel Ultra, Claude Code at ~97% on one 2026 benchmark) outrun the very providers in our research pool, and Perplexity's own product orchestrates 19 models internally ([FelloAI comparison](https://felloai.com/ai-search-deep-research-comparison/), [AIMultiple](https://aimultiple.com/ai-deep-research)). Our five-provider pool is an assembly of commodity parts whose differentiation decays as the parts improve.
4. **Debate's known failure mode is the one we haven't defended against.** Sycophantic convergence — heterogeneous panels converging unanimously on wrong answers via social reinforcement — is now a well-documented pathway ([Too Polite to Disagree, arXiv 2604.02668](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.02668); [Talk Isn't Always Cheap, arXiv 2509.05396](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.05396); [The Confident Liar, arXiv 2606.10296](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.10296)). Our Round-2 refeeds full anonymized peer content — exactly the topology those papers study — and our defenses (#19 framing defense, #110 isolation audit) are filed, not built.

### The strongest case FOR the direction

1. **Heterogeneity is the variant of debate that works.** The same literature that undercuts naive MAD consistently finds cross-model diversity is the ingredient that pays: heterogeneous ensembles beat homogeneous scaling, and "responses generated by heterogeneous models contribute significantly more than those produced by the same model" ([Understanding Agent Scaling via Diversity, arXiv 2602.03794](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.03794); [Mixture-of-Agents, arXiv 2406.04692](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.04692v1); [adaptive heterogeneous MAD, Springer 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s44443-025-00353-3)). ai-council is heterogeneity-first by construction — five labs, five epistemic cultures, blind-shuffled (ADR-02/ADR-03). Most peer frameworks run one backbone with different hats.
2. **Nobody else is building a *decision* engine.** The 2026 framework landscape (LangGraph, CrewAI, AG2, vendor SDKs) is workflow plumbing — state graphs, role-play crews, event buses ([framework comparisons](https://pecollective.com/blog/ai-agent-frameworks-compared/), [QubitTool 2026 showdown](https://qubittool.com/blog/ai-agent-framework-comparison-2026)). None ships an opinionated decision protocol: blind voting, non-participating synthesizer, minority reports, degradation alarms with exit-code semantics, transcript-free verdict packages, an ADR pipeline with binding governance. That whole layer (ADR-03/08/11/13, DRAFT-INT-1) is genuinely differentiated and is the part a foreign repo actually consumes.
3. **The adversarial-oversight literature backs the *shape* of the bet.** "It is harder to lie than to refute a lie" — debate-as-scalable-oversight results in 2026 show judge accuracy *improving* as debaters strengthen ([rewire.it debate overview](https://rewire.it/blog/ai-safety-via-debate/), [Collaborative Disagreement Resolution, arXiv 2607.01251](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.01251)). For `judge` mode — evaluating a proposal — an adversarial multi-model panel has theory on its side in a way single-model reflection does not.
4. **The cost bet is real and unduplicated.** CLI-subscription seats at $0 marginal (ADR-12, witnessed E2E) attack the one thing that makes debate-vs-single-model economics unfavorable. If a 5-seat debate costs what one API call costs, the matched-compute argument against debate loses most of its sting. No peer framework has this because no peer framework assumes an operator who already pays for four assistant subscriptions.

### Held in tension

The honest reading: the vision is right about **where** the value is (decisions, bias, grounding, governance) and unproven about **how** (this specific 2-round refeed topology). Two live positions, deliberately not resolved here:

- **Position 1 — keep the direction, buy the evidence.** The differentiation is the decision/governance layer plus heterogeneity plus $0 seats; run #55 (matched-compute baseline) and #110 (isolation audit) before any architectural swing, and let the data adjudicate.
- **Position 2 — reframe from "debate engine" to "adjudication engine."** Accept that generation-side debate may be commoditized/internalized by reasoning models, and concentrate the tool's identity on what survives that trend: heterogeneous *verification* (judge mode, refutation roles, evidence adjudication, structured dissent) rather than heterogeneous *generation*. The oversight literature supports exactly this half.

## 2. Holes — where the vision is under-specified, self-contradictory, or undelivered

- **H1 — "Creativity engine" exists in the mission frame, not in the record.** `VISION.md` says "multi-model AI debate and research tool for architectural decision-making" — decision engine, not creativity engine. No ADR mentions creativity. The nearest mechanism, `ideas` mode, is the *least* developed mode: 1 round (ADR-04), no divergence support, no creativity metric, no rubric (the `SYNTHESIS_QUALITY_RUBRIC` scores synthesis fidelity, not idea novelty). If creativity is a goal, it is entirely unbuilt; if it is a byproduct, the vision statement given to this audit overstates it. Two live fixes: (a) amend VISION to claim creativity explicitly and give `ideas` mode real mechanics (divergence rounds, semantic-diversity scoring — see the 2026 ideation literature in §3-M5), or (b) strike "creativity engine" from the self-description and own "decision engine" cleanly.
- **H2 — "Research when it doesn't know" is architecturally inverted.** The vision wants debate that reaches for evidence mid-flight. What exists is a hard fork: `research` mode *bypasses debate entirely* (ADR-05; ARCHITECTURE invariant 6 makes the isolation a stated invariant), and debate's only evidence organ is the #18 crux-check — one claim, one provider (`perplexity` only), 90s budget, 4,000-char evidence cap, fired once between rounds 1 and 2 (`config/settings.yaml:399-410`, `crux_check.py`). The config itself warns that widening it "is a cost decision that needs re-review, not a tuning knob." So the vision's central verb — *debate that researches* — is delivered at roughly 5% of its stated ambition, by design.
- **H3 — "Decide and act" has no act.** The loop ends at `council-verdict-*.json`. Everything after — reading the verdict, drafting the ADR, acting on it — is caller-side and manual: #38 (verdict→ADR read-back guide), #36 (authoring advisor), #37 (decomposition advisory) are all unbuilt \[S13]. ADR-11 §5 explicitly rejects a library API and MCP, so the "act" step lives in whatever the foreign repo's agent improvises. The contract's §8 walkthrough is honest about this — step 7 is "the caller's CC session drafts the ADR" — but the vision's "decision it can then act on" is currently a promissory note.
- **H4 — The delegation contract promises more than the research lane delivers.** ADR-11/ADR-13 stamp Contract-Version 1.0, yet a Lane-A *research* commission gets no verdict package at all (#34 — the earmarked 1.1 bump), the debate-path package can name a return copy that never landed (#76, `output.py:790-791`), and frontmatter `models:` is dead on every non-`--lite` run with the two entry points disagreeing (#69, `cli.py:607/:718-721`). None of this is hidden — the backlog is admirably honest — but "an external agent can commission the Council without ambiguity" (E1 theme) is not yet true for the exact question type (§4) the vision leads with.
- **H5 — "Non-cognitive-bias debate" defends against the wrong bias vector, so far.** ADR-03 anonymization removes *identity* anchoring (who said it). The 2026 failure mode is *content* sycophancy (that it was said at all): full peer-response refeed drives convergence regardless of attribution ([arXiv 2604.02668](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.02668)). The three planned defenses — #19 framing defense, #110 round-2 isolation audit, #55 baseline — are the actual bias program, and all three are open. Meanwhile the dissent signal itself is unreliable in both directions: #58 (minority report emitted despite consensus) and rubric item 2's hallucinated-consensus failure. Claiming "reduces cognitive bias" today rests on one shipped mechanism and one bounded crux-check.
- **H6 — The cost story contradicts itself at the margins.** ADR-12's CLI lane is shipped but `settings.yaml` declares zero `backend: cli` seats (verified during #66 — every live run bills API), the default flip is evidence-gated on the unrun #27 parity trial, and the crux-check now spends research money on *every* debate while `print_cost_summary` cannot see it (#82 — two unreconciled cost ledgers). "CLI seats to cut token cost" is true as capability, not yet true as configuration.
- **H7 — The crux artifact is deliberately invisible to the delegated caller.** #18 Phase A froze the contract: the crux artifact "must stay OUT of the verdict package" (`tests/test_output.py:1842`). Defensible as contract discipline (no 1.x bump inside Phase A), but it means the one epistemically distinguishing act the debate performs — grounding a disputed fact in retrieved evidence — is not visible in the machine-authoritative deliverable. A Lane-A caller cannot tell a grounded verdict from an ungrounded one without parsing the transcript, which the verdict package exists to spare it from. (Candidate rider on the #34/#76 Contract-Version 1.1 batch; or defend the exclusion explicitly in an ADR — both options live.)

## 3. What we're missing — 2026 capabilities not yet considered

Each: basis (web) · effect · rough cost · conflicts-with.

- **M1 — Structured vote aggregation instead of synthesizer-prose-only verdicts.** The field has moved past "one model reads the transcript and writes the answer": weighted/second-order aggregation (Optimal Weight, Inverse Surprising Popularity — [arXiv 2510.01499](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.01499v1)), electoral methods ([arXiv 2410.15168](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.15168)), and conformal social choice with abstention guarantees ([arXiv 2604.07667](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.07667)). Today the verdict is one synthesizer's reading — a single epistemic point of failure, and the root of the #58 dissent-extraction fragility (dissent is *inferred from prose* instead of *recorded as votes*). · **Effect:** verdict robustness; a machine-checkable dissent signal (fixes the #58 class structurally); judge-conformity resistance. · **Cost:** M — panelists emit a structured vote block alongside prose; an aggregation step before synthesis; synthesis demoted to rationale-writing. · **Conflicts:** ADR-01 (synthesizer as sole verdict author), `SYNTHESIS_QUALITY_RUBRIC`, the `_anonymize_responses()` contract stays intact.
- **M2 — Assigned adversarial roles (affirm/refute), not just symmetric critique.** Debate-driven claim verification assigns opposing stances (DebateCV — [arXiv 2507.19090](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.19090v3)); devil's-advocate systems measurably improve group decisions and psychological safety ([IUI 2024](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3640543.3645199), [arXiv 2502.06251](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.06251v1)); prover-estimator protocols give the refuter role theoretical teeth. Our rounds are symmetric critique — everyone politely reviews everyone — which is precisely the topology that drifts to consensus. The night council already pointed here (#19 option b, "framing-challenge role, shadow-mode first"). · **Effect:** structural counter to sycophantic convergence; sharper `judge` mode. · **Cost:** S–M — persona/prompt work in `settings.yaml`, one role-rotation rule; no new plumbing. · **Conflicts:** ADR-03's symmetric-anonymity spirit (roles reintroduce asymmetry — deliberately); ADR-02 panel semantics.
- **M3 — Retrieval as a first-class debate element, not a single mid-debate stitch.** 2026 fact-checking pipelines put *verified* evidence retrieval inside the deliberation loop (SLED's denoising retrieval + debate verification — [ACM WebConf 2026](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3774904.3792285); evidence-grounded advocates per corpus). Our crux-check is one claim, one provider, once. Live options, all costed differently: (a) multi-crux (top-N disputed claims), (b) a per-panelist bounded evidence-request budget ("tool-adjudication"), (c) an evidence-clerk role that retrieves for the panel on demand. · **Effect:** the vision's "researches when it doesn't know" verb becomes real inside decision modes. · **Cost:** M–L, plus real per-run spend (the settings.yaml warning is correct); #82 (cost-ledger reconciliation) becomes a prerequisite, not a nit. · **Conflicts:** ADR-05/invariant-6 research isolation; ADR-06 cost posture; the #18 Phase-A bounded design (would need a Phase B ADR).
- **M4 — An MCP adapter over the existing contract.** The field converged: MCP at ~97M monthly SDK downloads with every major provider adopting it, A2A at 150+ orgs, and the production pattern is "MCP client internally, A2A peer externally" ([Zuplo protocol stack](https://zuplo.com/blog/agent-protocol-stack-mcp-a2a-acp-2026), [Zylos convergence review](https://zylos.ai/research/2026-03-26-agent-interoperability-protocols-mcp-a2a-acp-convergence/)). ADR-11 rejected MCP when it was one option among three; in 2026 it is the lingua franca of exactly the "foreign repo asks the council" hand-off the vision describes. The CLI-as-ABI logic (stateless, exit codes, files) remains sound — which is why **both options stay live**: (a) hold CLI-as-ABI, document an MCP non-goal with a revisit trigger; (b) ship a *thin* MCP server that shells out to the same CLI and returns the verdict package — an adapter, not a second surface, so ADR-11's statelessness argument survives intact. · **Effect:** any MCP-speaking agent (Claude Code, Codex, vendor agents) can commission the Council without bespoke shell/parse code; the #36 advisor could ship *as prompts in the MCP tool description*. · **Cost:** S–M for the adapter; governance cost of amending ADR-11 §considered-and-rejected. · **Conflicts:** ADR-11 (explicitly), VISION "not embedded as a library" (arguably not violated by an adapter — contested, keep open).
- **M5 — Decision memory: precedent retrieval into the debate context.** LLM-ADR research shows a small recency window of prior ADRs materially improves decision quality ("Context Matters" — [arXiv 2604.03826](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.03826v1); DRAFT-ing ADDs — [arXiv 2504.08207](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.08207)). Our council debates every question amnesiac: no retrieval over `docs/decisions/`, hub ADRs, or LESSONS — so a verdict can silently contradict the installed governance base, and the caller only finds out at ADR-drafting time. Note the G4 disposition ("cross-session memory conflicts with ADR-03 — not pursued") closed *panelist calibration memory*; precedent-retrieval into the **question context** is a different mechanism that never touches panelist identity — the tension is real but not identical, decide it explicitly rather than by inheritance. · **Effect:** verdicts consistent with prior binding decisions; the caller's ADR-drafting step gets its citations for free; also the cheapest real step toward the "creativity" claim (ideas mode grounded in what was already tried). · **Cost:** M — a retrieval pre-step over a small curated corpus; no new providers. · **Conflicts:** G4 disposition (partially), ADR-03 (only if misbuilt), hub-boundary rules on reading `.dev-knowledge` corpora.

## 4. The research-and-decide loop — concrete trace

The vision's heart: a foreign repo asks "which library / which algorithm / which math approach" and gets back a grounded, bias-reduced decision. Trace against the shipped design:

1. **Author.** The caller's agent must produce a GUIDE-conformant brief from a 599-line `COUNCIL_QUESTION_GUIDE.md`. No template tooling exists (#36/#37 unbuilt) — the advisor layer the vision calls "the window on the world" is the least-built part of the chain. *Break B1: authoring is unassisted.*
2. **Mode fork — the archetypal question falls into a crack.** "Which library should we use?" is simultaneously a research question (needs current facts: maintenance status, benchmarks, licenses) and a pick question (needs a decision). The mode system forces a fork (ADR-04, invariant 6):
   - **Route research:** the pool runs, five providers, citations — but *no debate, no blind voting, no bias reduction, no verdict package* (#34). The caller gets a merged report and must decide alone. The "bias-reduced decision" half evaporates. Exit code 3 covers provider degradation — nothing covers "you asked for a decision and got a reading list."
   - **Route pick:** the panel debates from *parametric memory* — five models' training-data beliefs about libraries, exactly where stale knowledge and popularity bias live. Fresh evidence enters only if (a) Round 1 happens to produce a *single* central empirical disagreement, (b) the extractor (synthesizer, `openai`) names it under a well-formed `## Crux` heading, (c) perplexity answers within 90s, (d) 4,000 chars of evidence suffice. Multi-crux questions — which library questions usually are (performance AND maintenance AND license) — get at most one crux checked; the rest of the verdict rests ungrounded. *Break B2: the flagship use case gets either research-without-decision or decision-with-one-fact-checked.*
3. **Crux-check failure is safe but silent-ish.** `crux_check.py` never raises into the debate (good), and `no_empirical_crux` / `retrieval_unavailable` are honestly distinguished (the terra passes earned that). But the outcome lands only in `DebateOutcome.crux` and the metrics sidecar — **the verdict package excludes the crux artifact by Phase-A design** (`tests/test_output.py:1842`, §2-H7). *Break B3: the delegated caller cannot distinguish a grounded verdict from an ungrounded one without the transcript.*
4. **Verdict and return.** Here the chain is genuinely strong: deterministic artifact names, exit-code semantics, fail-loud routing, transcript-free verdict package with dissent pointer (ADR-08/10/11/13, DRAFT-INT-1). The I/O shell of the loop is delivery-grade. Residuals #75/#76/#78 are honest edge-case debt, not design holes.
5. **Act.** Manual (§2-H3). The verdict→ADR path is a documented walkthrough, not a mechanism; nothing closes the loop back ("was the decision acted on? did it hold?") — no adoption-evidence signal ever returns to the council. *Break B4: decide-and-act ends at decide; B5: the run's true cost (debate + retrieval) isn't even visible in one ledger (#82).*

**Verdict on the loop:** the *edges* (invocation in, artifacts out) are the best-engineered part of the repo; the *middle* (evidence-grounded, bias-reduced deciding) is delivered at prototype depth — one crux, one provider, invisible to the caller; and the *fork* between research and debate means the vision's headline question type is served by neither lane whole. Two live remedies, not converged: (a) a composed "grounded-pick" flow (research pool seeds an evidence pack → debate over it — see §5 Swing A); (b) keep the lanes pure and make the *caller-side advisor* (#36–#38) orchestrate research→pick as two commissions, which needs no architecture change at all — the contract already supports sequencing.

## 5. One big swing

If one architectural bet could change to serve the vision, two candidates — deliberately left as a fork:

**Swing A — collapse the research/debate fork: every decision run is evidence-first.**
Make retrieval a first-class stage of decision modes: research pool (bounded, cached) produces a cited evidence pack → the pack is in every Round-1 prompt → debate argues *over evidence* instead of *from memory* → crux-check remains as the mid-debate top-up → verdict package carries the evidence manifest + crux status. `research` mode survives as the evidence stage running solo.
*What it buys:* the vision's central sentence becomes literally true; the archetypal library/algorithm question gets one lane instead of a crack; parametric-memory bias (the bias that matters most for "which X" questions) is attacked at the root; #34 parity mostly dissolves because there is one path.
*What it trades:* ADR-04's mode system and ADR-05's isolation invariant (a deliberate, load-bearing simplification); per-run cost rises materially (ADR-06 posture, the settings.yaml cost warning); latency; #82 must be fixed first or spend becomes invisible at scale; the research subsystem's blast radius enters every debate (ADR-08-class degradation semantics needed on the debate path).

**Swing B — replace the prose-synthesized verdict with structured votes + aggregation; demote synthesis to rationale.**
Panelists emit machine-readable positions (option, confidence, cruxes, dissent) beside prose; an aggregation layer (weighted/second-order/conformal — §3-M1) computes the verdict and the *measured* dissent; the synthesizer writes the rationale for a verdict it no longer solely authors.
*What it buys:* removes the single-reader epistemic bottleneck; makes dissent a datum instead of an extraction (#58 dies structurally); resistance to judge-conformity documented in 2026; the verdict package's `decision` field becomes computed, not narrated — a stronger delegation promise than any plumbing fix.
*What it trades:* ADR-01 (synthesizer selection becomes rationale-author selection — the Epic-B evidence base partially re-opens); the rubric re-scopes; structured self-reports are gameable/miscalibrated in their own way; prose nuance ("option A, but only if constraint X falls") is harder to aggregate than to narrate.

Not chosen as the swing, with reasons on record: an MCP surface (§3-M4) is an adapter question, not an architecture bet — the binding constraint is epistemic, not plumbing; and "become a creativity engine" (§2-H1) is a *vision-record* decision the operator must make before any architecture should chase it.
